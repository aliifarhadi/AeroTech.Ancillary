## Source reality to preserve
Existing `AncillaryReservation` aggregate holds an overall `Reference`, `OrderId`, `IdempotencyKey`, `ExpiresAt`, status and `Units`. `AncillaryReservationUnit` stores `OrderServiceId`, `ServiceDefinitionId`, `ProvisionId`, `TravellerId?`, `CoverageScope`, `CoveredFlightIds`, `Quantity`, `StockPoolId?`, `ProviderUnitRef?`, status and `CancellationReasonCode?`. Existing APIs currently Hold/Get/Confirm; this is not sufficient proof of operational completeness. No cleanup in phases 1/2.

## Data design gate (not frozen until real source audit)
- Existing reservation aggregate is the natural starting candidate, not a mandate for new Reservation aggregate. Keep overall `ReservationReference` plus per-unit `ProviderUnitRef`, relevant `StockPoolId` and `FulfillmentProviderKey` bound to each service. Avoid invented `ReservationPreparation`, generic orchestration engine and accepted-snapshot clones.
- Determine whether StockPool owns a hold/assignment ledger or Reservation owns reservation units based on atomicity and allocation behavior observed in actual FlightFlow/provider code; do not duplicate authoritative live capacity or create FK cycles.
- Commerce snapshot safety: references to published PricingId/ProvisionId/ServiceDefinitionId and resolved quoted amount must remain immutable once accepted by Ordering. Who owns authoritative accepted snapshot is an explicit Ordering boundary decision, not a Phase 1 task.
- Define supplier capability matrix at Phase 3 with actual evidence: Reserve/DirectIssue/Cancel/Refund/PartialCancel/TTL, not speculative common enum values. Payment/EMD/settlement rules remain owned by appropriate services.
